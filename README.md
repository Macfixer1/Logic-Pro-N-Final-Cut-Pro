THE COMPLETE HISTORY OF LOGIC PRO & FINAL CUT PRO FREE TRIALS (2002–2026)
A long-form archival narrative of how Apple gave creators unprecedented access… and then took it away.
---
1. The Origins — When Apple First Introduced Trials (Early 2000s → 2019)
Apple’s pro apps — Final Cut Pro (video) and Logic Pro (music) — have always been premium, one‑time‑purchase tools. For years, Apple offered short 30‑day trials, mostly hidden on their website, intended for studios and professionals evaluating the software.
These early trials were:
• 30 days
• Full-featured
• Downloadable directly from Apple
• Resettable only by installing major new versions
They were not widely advertised, but they existed as a courtesy for professionals.
---
2. The Pandemic Shift — Apple’s Most Generous Era (2020–2022)
When the world went into lockdown, Apple made a historic move:
The 90‑Day Free Trial Era Begins
Apple extended both Logic Pro and Final Cut Pro trials from 30 days → 90 days, giving creators three full months to:
• learn editing
• produce full albums
• complete films
• build workflows
• test plugins
• explore the entire ecosystem
This was the most generous trial period Apple had ever offered.
Creators loved it. Many careers started because of these trials.
---
3. The Golden Age Continues (2023–Mid‑2025)
For several years, Apple kept the 90‑day trials alive.
The links were:
• apple.com/final-cut-pro/trial
• apple.com/logic-pro/trial
You could download the full installers, run them for 90 days, and — as Cult of Mac later noted — some users even discovered that installing a newer version would reset the trial window for another 90 days. 
This wasn’t officially supported, but it worked.
---
4. The Turning Point — Apple Creator Studio Announced (January 2026)
Everything changed in January 2026.
Apple launched Apple Creator Studio, a subscription bundle including:
• Final Cut Pro
• Logic Pro
• Pixelmator Pro
• Motion
• Compressor
• MainStage
• Premium content for Keynote, Pages, Numbers
• New AI-powered features
The subscription cost:
• $12.99/month
• $129/year
• Student pricing: $2.99/month 
This bundle replaced the old trial system.
---
5. Apple Quietly Removes the Free Trials (January 13–16, 2026)
According to Cult of Mac, Apple killed the standalone 90‑day trials immediately after launching Creator Studio.
They removed:
• the download links
• the trial pages
• all references to the free trials
• the ability to reset trials by updating versions
This was confirmed on January 16, 2026. 
From that moment on:
The 90‑day trials were officially dead.
---
6. The New Reality — Only a 30‑Day Trial Through Creator Studio
Apple replaced the old trials with:
• 30‑day trial of Apple Creator Studio
• 90‑day trial only if you buy a new Mac or iPad (device‑based promo) 
This meant:
• No more standalone Logic Pro trial
• No more standalone Final Cut Pro trial
• No more 90‑day downloads
• No more direct installers
• No more trial resets
To try either app, you must now subscribe to Creator Studio. 
---
7. The Strange Aftermath — Some Trial Links Still Worked (January–February 2026)
Even after Apple removed the main trial pages, regional versions of the old trial links remained active for a short time.
9to5Mac confirmed that:
• The US trial links were dead
• But UK, Italy, and other regions still had working 90‑day trial downloads
• Apple had not yet removed them globally
• You could still download, install, and run the full 90‑day trials if you found the right regional link
This was reported on January 19, 2026. 
However:
By February 2, 2026, Apple pulled the remaining regional links.
9to5Mac updated their article to confirm this. 
This marked the final end of the standalone trials.
---
8. Why Apple Removed the Trials
Based on the reporting:
1. To push Creator Studio subscriptions
Apple wanted a unified subscription model similar to Adobe Creative Cloud.
The trials were removed to funnel users into the new service. 
2. To stop the “trial reset” loophole
Users could previously reinstall newer versions to get another 90 days.
Apple closed this loophole by removing the installers entirely. 
3. To simplify marketing
Creator Studio became the single entry point for all pro apps.
---
9. What You Can Get Now (2026 → Present)
As of now, the only official ways to try Logic Pro or Final Cut Pro are:
Option A — Apple Creator Studio (30‑day trial)
You get:
• Final Cut Pro
• Logic Pro
• Pixelmator Pro
• Motion
• Compressor
• MainStage
• Premium content
• AI features
This is the only universal trial available.
(Confirmed by PCMag and Cult of Mac.) 
Option B — Buy a new Mac or iPad (90‑day trial)
Apple gives a 3‑month trial of Creator Studio with new hardware purchases.
(Confirmed by Apple’s own Creator Studio page.) 
Option C — Buy the apps outright
Apple still sells:
• Final Cut Pro — $299.99 £300 approx.
• Logic Pro — $199.99 / £200 approx.
These are one‑time purchases.
(Confirmed by PCMag.) 
---
10. The “You Can Get Them Here!” Part — What That Means Today
Since Apple removed the standalone installers, the only legitimate place to get the apps or trials is:
→ Apple Creator Studio (official)
This is now the central hub for all trials and downloads.
(Confirmed by Apple and all reporting.) 
There are no legal standalone trial installers anymore.
